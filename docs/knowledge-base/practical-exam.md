# The practical driving exam (*praktische Prüfung*)

**Who this is written for.** A holder of an Argentine class B car licence, resident in Berlin, converting it under the *Umschreibung* (conversion) route. Argentina does not appear in *Anlage 11 FeV* (the country annex listing states whose licences may be converted without an exam), so this route requires **both** German exams — theory and practical (FeV § 31 Abs. 2 in conjunction with § 15 Abs. 1). An EU passport makes no difference here: what governs is the state that **issued the licence**, not your nationality. Had the licence itself been an EU one, no exam and no conversion procedure of this kind would arise at all.

**Verification status.** This document draws on two research files. `ar-exams-practical.json` quotes the primary law verbatim and carries per-claim confidence ratings; `practical-exam.json` is broader but was researched for a first-time-licence persona. Neither has been through a verification pass — no `practical-exam-verification.json` exists. Where the two disagree I say so and explain which I trust. Where the research itself flags a claim as low or medium confidence, or leaves it open, it is marked **⚠️ unverified** and must not be relied on without a direct check.

---

## Bottom line

- The practical exam is **55 minutes total, of which at least 30 minutes is pure driving time** (*Anlage 7 Nr. 2.3 FeV*). There is **no shortened version for conversion candidates** — the only statutory 10-minute reduction is reserved for lifting an automatic-transmission restriction or removing code 197.
- **The 12 *Sonderfahrten* (mandatory special lessons) do not apply to you.** FeV § 31 Abs. 2 switches off the training rules entirely on the conversion route. There is no legal minimum number of lessons at all — but your *Fahrlehrer* (driving instructor) decides when you are presented for the exam, and a driving school is legally unavoidable regardless (StVG § 2 Abs. 15).
- **The practical exam is in German only, and no interpreter is permitted.** The 12-foreign-language option in *Anlage 7 Nr. 1.3 FeV* is drafted for the theory exam alone; nothing equivalent exists for the practical. Your instructor sits in the car but may **not** translate the examiner's instructions.
- Official fee: **€129.83 per attempt** (€109.10 net plus 19 % VAT, *GebOSt* Anlage Nr. 402.3), unchanged since 31 January 2024, plus your driving school's own *Vorstellungsentgelt* (presentation fee) of roughly **€180–230 in Berlin** — charged again on every retake.
- Retakes: minimum **two weeks** between attempts (FeV § 18 Abs. 1), no cap on the number of attempts, but the practical must be passed **within 12 months of the theory pass** (§ 18 Abs. 2), and Berlin grants **no extension** of an expiring *Prüfungszulassung*.
- **37 % of class B candidates failed the practical exam in 2025** — an all-time high (TÜV-Verband, 11 February 2026). The failures that catch experienced foreign drivers are not car control but German-specific rules: *rechts vor links*, the double *Schulterblick* (shoulder check), yielding to straight-on cyclists when turning, and driving too slowly.
- A federal reform adopted by cabinet on **20 May 2026** would cut the exam to 40 minutes total / 25 minutes driving, targeted for **early 2027 — but it is still a draft, and the date is disputed**. Waiting is not advisable (see §19).

## 1. Why you must sit this exam at all

Argentina is absent from *Anlage 11 FeV*, verified by full-text download of the annex on 4 August 2026. The annex covers roughly 60 jurisdictions — Switzerland, Japan, Singapore, South Korea, Taiwan, Ukraine, all US states, all Canadian provinces, all Australian states, and others — but **no South American state**. That absence closes off the § 31 Abs. 1 exemptions, which are the only route to waiving the *Befähigungsprüfung* (competence examination). What remains is § 31 Abs. 2:

> Beantragt der Inhaber einer Fahrerlaubnis aus einem nicht in Anlage 11 aufgeführten Staat unter den Voraussetzungen des Absatzes 1 Satz 1 und 2 die Erteilung einer Fahrerlaubnis für die entsprechende Klasse von Kraftfahrzeugen, sind die Vorschriften über die Ausbildung nicht anzuwenden.
> — FeV § 31 Abs. 2

Only *die Vorschriften über die Ausbildung* — the rules on **training** — are disapplied. The examination itself stands, in full:

> Der Bewerber um eine Fahrerlaubnis hat seine Befähigung in einer theoretischen und einer praktischen Prüfung nachzuweisen.
> — FeV § 15 Abs. 1

There is no reduced *Befähigungsprüfung* variant for foreign-licence holders anywhere in § 15; the reductions in § 15 Abs. 2–4 concern other licence classes (L, BE, C1E, DE, D1E, A upgrades) and are irrelevant to a class B conversion. ADAC independently confirms the split, naming Argentina explicitly alongside the USA and Canada as countries requiring both exams while noting that no driving-school training is required.

**The practical consequence worth internalising: you sit the identical exam a German 18-year-old sits.** Being an experienced driver earns you no procedural concession — only, potentially, fewer lessons.

## 2. Format and duration

*Anlage 7 Nr. 2.3 FeV* sets the clock:

> Klasse B — 55 Minuten […] davon Fahrzeit — 30 Minuten
>
> Bei Prüfungen, die ausschließlich der Aufhebung der Beschränkung einer Fahrerlaubnis auf das Führen von Kraftfahrzeugen mit Automatikgetriebe (§ 17a Absatz 2) oder der Austragung der Schlüsselzahl 197 (§ 17a Absatz 4) dienen, verkürzt sich die Dauer der praktischen Prüfung um 10 Minuten.
> — *Anlage 7 Nr. 2.3 FeV*

| Item | Value | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Total exam duration, class B | 55 minutes | *Anlage 7 Nr. 2.3 FeV* |
| Minimum pure driving time (*Fahrzeit*) | 30 minutes | *Anlage 7 Nr. 2.3 FeV* |
| Shortening for conversion candidates | **None** | *Anlage 7 Nr. 2.3 FeV* (reduction limited to § 17a Abs. 2 / Abs. 4 cases) |
| Regime in force | *Optimierte praktische Fahrerlaubnisprüfung* (OPFEP), since 1 January 2021 | previously 45 min total / ~20–25 min driving |

The footnote to Nr. 2.3 defines what the 30 minutes excludes, and it matters: *Fahrzeit* runs **without** the *Grundfahraufgaben* (basic manoeuvres), without the safety checks, and without preparation and wrap-up such as announcing the result. So the 55 minutes is the envelope; the 30 minutes is the part where you are driving in traffic. The footnote adds that this pure driving time corresponds to EU requirements — which is why it will not shrink much even under reform.

Practically: expect roughly 5–10 minutes of paperwork and safety checks, then a continuous drive of 35–45 minutes with the three manoeuvres interleaved, then the return to the *Prüfstelle* (test centre) and the verdict. **The drive back to the test centre is part of the exam** — a classic point where concentration lapses.

## 3. Before you drive off

Two things happen before the wheels move, and both are assessed.

### 3.1 *Fahrtechnische Vorbereitung* (technical preparation)

Per *Prüfungsrichtlinie praktische Prüfung* Teil A Nr. 1.4.8.3.1, you must attend to, unprompted: correct seat adjustment **including the head restraint** and, where adjustable, the steering wheel; the seat belt; correct mirror adjustment; and properly closed doors. You must be familiar with the controls. If you use driver-assistance systems, you must operate them yourself (*"Werden Assistenzsysteme benutzt, so muss er diese eigenständig bedienen."*).

The head restraint is the item experienced drivers skip. Do it visibly.

### 3.2 The three *Sicherheitskontrollen* (safety checks)

**Class B has no *Abfahrtkontrolle*.** That full pre-departure inspection is reserved by *Anlage 7 Nr. 2.1.2 FeV* for classes C, C1, D, D1 and T. What class B has instead is a sampled safety check, and the *Prüfungsrichtlinie* is unambiguous that it happens **every time**:

> In den Klassen A, A2, A1, AM und B sind in jeder Prüfung vor Fahrtantritt die folgenden Sicherheitskontrollen stichprobenartig (drei Prüfpunkte aus verschiedenen Themenbereichen) durchzuführen.
> — *Prüfungsrichtlinie praktische Prüfung* Teil A Nr. 1.4.8.3.2.1

**Exactly three check points, drawn from different topic areas, in every exam.** Note a conflict here: `practical-exam.json` describes the examiner as sampling "up to 3" checks. The verbatim *Prüfungsrichtlinie* text quoted above says *drei Prüfpunkte* as a mandatory feature of every exam. The verbatim primary source wins — plan on three, always.

The catalogue you can be asked from:

| # | Topic area | What you may be asked to do |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Reifen* (tyres) | Damage, tread depth (*Profiltiefe*), tyre pressure |
| 2 | *Scheinwerfer, Leuchten, Blinker, Hupe* (headlights, lamps, indicators, horn) | Switch on/off and check function of side lights, dipped beam, high beam, tail lights with number-plate illumination, rear fog light, hazard warning lights, indicators, horn, brake lights; plus **name the warning lights** (*Kontrollleuchten benennen*) |
| 3 | *Rückstrahler* (reflectors) | Presence, damage |
| 4 | *Lenkung* (steering) | Release the steering lock |
| 5 | *Bremsanlage* (brakes) | Function check of the service brake and the parking brake |
| 6 | *Flüssigkeitsstände* (fluid levels) | Engine oil, coolant, washer fluid |

*Source: Prüfungsrichtlinie praktische Prüfung Teil A Nr. 1.4.8.3.2.1, pp. 13–14 (verbatim list).* Items 5 and 6 are noted in the source as class B specific.

This is the part of the exam where German vocabulary is unavoidable and also entirely learnable — it is a closed list of six areas. Learn the nouns. See §13 for the phrasing.

## 4. The three *Grundfahraufgaben*

*Anlage 7 Nr. 2.1.4.2 FeV* fixes both the tasks and the arithmetic: exactly three are driven, one from group (a) and two from group (b). The examiner chooses which.

| Group | Official name (*Anlage 7 Nr. 2.1.4.2 FeV*) | English |
|---|---|---|
| **(a) — one of these** | *Fahren nach rechts rückwärts unter Ausnutzung einer Einmündung, Kreuzung oder Einfahrt* (a) aa) | Reversing to the right around a corner, using a side road, junction or driveway |
| | *Rückwärtsfahren in eine Parklücke, Längsaufstellung* (a) bb) | Reverse parallel parking |
| **(b) — two of these** | *Umkehren* (b) aa) | Turning the car around (three-point turn or equivalent) |
| | *Einfahren in eine Parklücke, Quer- oder Schrägaufstellung* (b) bb) | Bay parking, perpendicular or angled |
| | *Abbremsen mit höchstmöglicher Verzögerung* (b) cc) | Braking with maximum possible deceleration (*Gefahrbremsung*, emergency stop) |

> Summe der zu fahrenden Grundfahraufgaben: drei.
> — *Anlage 7 Nr. 2.1.4.2 FeV*

`practical-exam.json` adds a practice observation: **the *Gefahrbremsung* is almost always among the selected tasks.** This is the single task that most reliably trips experienced drivers, because habit says brake smoothly. The task requires genuinely maximum deceleration — hard enough to engage ABS. Braking politely fails it.

The manoeuvres are driven off the flowing traffic and, per the *Fahrzeit* footnote, do not count toward the 30 minutes of driving time.

## 5. The drive: route composition and what is guaranteed to be tested

Route composition is prescribed:

> Etwa die Hälfte der Fahrzeit soll für Prüfungsstrecken außerhalb geschlossener Ortschaften, möglichst auch unter Einschluss der Autobahnen oder Kraftfahrstraßen […] verwendet werden.
> — *Anlage 7 Nr. 2.4 FeV*

So roughly half your driving time should be outside built-up areas, ideally including *Autobahn* or *Kraftfahrstraße* (expressway) where one is reachable. From a Berlin *Prüfstelle* that means urban roads plus a higher-speed section.

The exam must be taken where you live: FeV § 17 Abs. 3 requires the exam at the place of the *Hauptwohnung* (principal residence) or place of work or study. For a Berlin resident, a Berlin *Prüfort*.

The *Prüfungsrichtlinie*'s *Prüfort* catalogue (Teil A Nr. 1.5, pp. 14–15) defines what a test location must be **capable of testing** — effectively a guaranteed-content list, and the best training checklist you will find:

- Pulling out into moving traffic from the kerb
- Roads carrying at least 100 vehicles/hour
- One-way streets including a left turn
- Lane changes away from junctions
- Multi-lane single-direction roads
- Approaching and crossing pedestrian crossings
- Passing public-transport stops
- **Junctions governed by *rechts vor links***
- Merging into priority roads
- Junctions with sign 206 (*Stop*)
- Light-controlled junctions
- Left turns against oncoming traffic
- **Right and left turns with cyclists or e-scooter riders on cycle tracks or hard shoulders**
- Junctions with *abknickende Vorfahrt* (priority road that bends)
- Out-of-town bends and restricted-visibility points
- Out-of-town overtaking opportunities
- Space for the *Grundfahraufgaben* away from flowing traffic
- A motorway within reach

The two bolded items are precisely the two things an Argentine driving habit is most likely to get wrong (see §18). They are not a matter of luck — the catalogue guarantees they will come up.

## 6. Assessment: the electronic protocol and the feedback sheet

Since 1 January 2021 the examiner records the entire exam on a tablet in a nationwide **electronic examination protocol** (*elektronisches Prüfprotokoll*, ePP), rating each driving task against catalogue criteria drawn from the *Fahraufgabenkatalog*. The design intent is a holistic *Fahrkompetenzbewertung* (driving-competence assessment) rather than tallying isolated errors.

Assessment runs across five cross-cutting observation categories (*Prüfungsrichtlinie* Teil A Nr. 1.6):

| # | *Beobachtungskategorie* | English |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Verkehrsbeobachtung* | Traffic observation |
| 2 | *Fahrzeugpositionierung* | Vehicle positioning |
| 3 | *Geschwindigkeitsanpassung* | Speed adaptation |
| 4 | *Kommunikation* | Communication (signalling, eye contact) |
| 5 | *Fahrzeugbedienung / Umweltbewusste Fahrweise* | Vehicle operation / environmentally aware driving |

Two of these are worth pausing on. **Category 4 means signalling is scored in its own right** — habitually not indicating when changing lanes, pulling out of a parking space or leaving a roundabout is a scored competence gap, independent of whether anyone was endangered. The legal hooks are StVO § 9 Abs. 1 Satz 1 (*"rechtzeitig und deutlich ankündigen"*) and § 5 Abs. 4a. **Category 3 cuts both ways** — see §18.4.

The result and the paperwork are also regulated. Per *Anlage 7 Nr. 2.6 FeV* the result is announced immediately at the end of the exam, and the candidate receives a written *Leistungsrückmeldung* (performance feedback) *mit Empfehlungen zum Fahrkompetenzerwerb* — recommendations for building driving competence. `practical-exam.json` reports that the sheet carries a **QR code linking to individualised improvement suggestions** on the pfep.de portal. You get this whether you pass or fail; if you fail, it is the most useful document you will hold, because it tells your instructor exactly what to drill.

⚠️ **Unverified:** whether a passing candidate in Berlin receives the licence card from the examiner on the spot (*Direktaushändigung*) or must collect it from the LABO. `practical-exam.json` lists this as an open question and notes practice varies. Ask your driving school.

## 7. Instant fail versus minor faults

*Anlage 7 Nr. 2.5.2 FeV* gives two failure routes: errors causing immediate termination of the exam, or *"die Wiederholung oder Häufung von leichten oder schweren Fehlern"* — the repetition or accumulation of light or serious faults.

### Instant-fail errors

From `practical-exam.json`, drawing on the *Prüfungsrichtlinie* and driving-school commentary:

| Error | Note |
|---|---|
| Endangering or damaging persons or property — including any case where the examiner or instructor has to intervene on the pedals or steering | The clearest bright line |
| Taking right of way from another road user (gross *Vorfahrt* violation) | See §18.1 |
| Running a red light | |
| Failing to stop at a stop sign | A rolling stop is not a stop |
| Violating a regulatory sign in a way that creates a hazard | |
| Illegal overtaking / ignoring an overtaking ban | |
| Passing a bus displaying hazard lights at more than walking pace | StVO § 20 Abs. 3/4 |
| Turning left into the oncoming lane | |
| Changing lanes without mirror check and *Schulterblick* | See §18.3 |
| No adequate reaction to vulnerable road users — children, elderly people, cyclists, people with disabilities | StVO § 3 Abs. 2a |
| Repeated significant speeding, or driving far too slowly / persistent hesitation | Both directions count |

### Minor faults

Isolated, less severe errors — imperfect gear selection, stalling once or twice, a slightly wide turn, a parking manoeuvre that is imperfect but corrected safely, momentary hesitancy — are recorded in the ePP but **can be compensated by otherwise good, safe, independent driving**. The *Prüfungsrichtlinie* says so explicitly:

> Vorschriften sind nicht kleinlich auszulegen; auch gute Leistungen sind zu berücksichtigen.
> — *Prüfungsrichtlinie praktische Prüfung* Teil A Nr. 1.6

("Rules are not to be applied pettily; good performance is also to be taken into account.")

What kills you is **accumulation within the same fault class**. Three separate missed shoulder checks is not three minor faults; it is a demonstrated observation deficit.

One further failure route that has nothing to do with you: *Anlage 7 Nr. 2.5.3 FeV* provides that the **driving instructor's** conduct can cause the exam to be failed — if the instructor's behaviour makes assessment of the candidate impossible, or attempts to deceive the examiner. This is the provision that makes translation during the drive dangerous (see §12).

## 8. Ending the drive: *fahrtechnischer Abschluss*

The end of the drive is scored too (*Prüfungsrichtlinie* Teil A Nr. 1.4.8.4). You must park in a traffic-compliant way; secure the car against rolling by engaging a gear and/or the parking brake — **double securing is required on a gradient**; secure it against unauthorised use; and:

> Beobachtung des Verkehrs vor und beim Öffnen der Tür; insbesondere auf von hinten nahende Verkehrsteilnehmer (z. B. Radfahrer oder Fahrer von Elektrokleinstfahrzeugen) ist zu achten.
> — *Prüfungsrichtlinie praktische Prüfung* Teil A Nr. 1.4.8.4

The "Dutch reach" — looking for approaching cyclists and e-scooters before opening your door — is formally assessed. In Berlin this is not a formality; dooring is a real hazard category here.

## 9. Mandatory training — and why it does not apply to you

### 9.1 What the law requires of a first-time candidate

For a first-time class B applicant there is **no legal minimum number of ordinary practice lessons** (*Übungsstunden*) — the instructor judges readiness under an *Ausbildungsplan* (FahrschAusbO § 6). What **is** legally fixed is the 12 *Sonderfahrten* (mandatory special drives):

| *Sonderfahrt* type | Units | English |
|---|---|---|
| *Überlandfahrten* | 5 | Out-of-town drives |
| *Autobahnfahrten* | 4 | Motorway drives |
| *Nachtfahrten* | 3 | Night drives |
| **Total** | **12 × 45 min** | |

*Legal basis: FahrschAusbO § 5 Abs. 2 and Anlage 4 (class B Grundausbildung).*

Germany-wide, a first-time candidate averages 33–45 driving lessons in total; in big cities such as Berlin and Hamburg around 45 is typical, while rural learners often manage on 25–30. Berlin averages roughly €56 per 45-minute lesson, with a full first licence costing around €2,800–4,000. **All of these figures describe the first-time-licence persona, not you.**

### 9.2 Why none of it binds you

FeV § 31 Abs. 2 disapplies *die Vorschriften über die Ausbildung* in their entirety. That means the theory-lesson units of the *Fahrschüler-Ausbildungsordnung*, the *Grundausbildung*, and **the 12 *Sonderfahrten* all fall away**. ADAC states it plainly for non-*Anlage 11* countries: *"Sie müssen jedoch keine Fahrschulausbildung durchlaufen."* A Berlin school specialising in conversions (asphalt-profis) says the same: there is no legal requirement as to how many lessons you must take. **Zero lessons is legally possible.**

Two things nonetheless make a driving school unavoidable:

> Wer zur Ausbildung, zur Ablegung der Prüfung oder zur Begutachtung der Eignung oder Befähigung ein Kraftfahrzeug auf öffentlichen Straßen führt, muss dabei von einem Fahrlehrer oder einem Fahrlehreranwärter im Sinne des Fahrlehrergesetzes begleitet werden.
> — StVG § 2 Abs. 15

A *Fahrlehrer* must be in the car for the exam. And the car itself must be a school car: *Anlage 7 Nr. 2.2.16 FeV* requires class B test vehicles to have acoustically or optically monitorable **dual pedal controls** (*Doppelbedienungseinrichtungen*), an additional interior mirror and two right-hand exterior mirrors, plus seats for examiner, instructor and candidate. (*Anlage 7 Nr. 2.2.17* also requires the *Fahrschule* L-plate marking to be removed for the exam drive.) You cannot bring your own car.

There is a useful side effect of StVG § 2 Abs. 15: because the instructor counts as the driver where the candidate holds no corresponding licence, **exam and lesson drives are not *Fahren ohne Fahrerlaubnis*** under StVG § 21 — even after your Argentine licence's six-month entitlement has lapsed. Driving to the test alone in your own car after that point would be.

### 9.3 How many lessons in practice

There is no authoritative number. The concrete published guidance found:

| Source | Guidance | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Fahrschule Hoffmann (Berlin) | *"approx. 4 appointments of 90 minutes each if you already have a lot of driving experience"* — about 8 × 45 min, ~6 hours behind the wheel | ⚠️ medium — most concrete number found |
| AS Fahrschule Berlin | *"Es sind keine Mindest- oder Pflichtstunden erforderlich"*; the number depends on how quickly you master German traffic rules | high (for the no-minimum claim) |
| asphalt-profis Berlin | A school is not legally required but strongly recommended; the instructor estimates the needed lessons after your first drives | ⚠️ medium |

**Realistic planning range: 6–10 × 45-minute units** for an experienced driver who must learn German-specific rules and the exam choreography. The research explicitly marks this as a derived estimate, not a quoted figure. Berlin lesson rates for the conversion track run €58.50–72 per 45 minutes, with English-language instruction typically costing about €9 more per lesson.

The decision is not really yours: schools will not present you until the *Fahrlehrer* judges you ready.

## 10. Automatic versus manual: B197 and code 78

This choice is made before you book, and getting it wrong restricts your licence across the EU.

| Route | What it means | Legal basis |
|---|---|---|
| **Exam on a manual car** | Unrestricted class B. No code. | — |
| **Exam on an automatic, no B197** | **EU code 78** entered — licence restricted to automatic-transmission vehicles. A real restriction, recognised EU-wide. | *Anlage 9 FeV* / EU code 78 |
| **B197 route** | Learn and take the exam on an automatic, but complete **at least 10 × 45 min lessons on a manual** at the driving school **plus a test drive (*Testfahrt*) of at least 15 minutes** on a manual, inside and outside built-up areas. The school certifies this; there is no official manual exam. *Schlüsselzahl 197* is entered in field 12. | FeV § 17a, in force since 1 April 2021 |

**Code 197 is informational, not a restriction.** It permits manual and automatic cars and is fully valid across the EU and abroad as a normal class B licence — precisely because code 78 is absent. The *Testfahrt* is an internal driving-school check, not part of the DEKRA/TÜV exam. Extra cost is roughly €30 in admin plus the manual lessons themselves.

Two caveats:

- **Code 78 can be lifted later**, either via the B197 procedure (10 lessons plus test drive) or by taking a fresh practical exam on a manual. Such an exam is 10 minutes shorter (*Anlage 7 Nr. 2.3 FeV*) and, per the *GebOSt* note to Nr. 402, correspondingly cheaper — but it is a whole extra procedure. Cheaper to get it right first time.
- **B197 only clears class B.** If you later extend to BE, C1 or C and take those exams on automatics, code 78 attaches to those classes.

⚠️ **Not addressed by the research, and it matters to you specifically:** whether the B197 route is available at all to a § 31 Abs. 2 conversion candidate. FeV § 17a requires *Ausbildung* of at least ten manual lessons — and § 31 Abs. 2 disapplies the training rules. Whether that means the requirement falls away (making B197 trivially available) or that B197 has no hook to attach to is not answered by either source file. If you intend to take the exam on an automatic, ask the LABO and your school in writing before booking: *"Ist der Weg über § 17a FeV (Schlüsselzahl 197) auch für einen Bewerber nach § 31 Abs. 2 FeV möglich, und welchen Nachweis verlangt die Behörde dafür?"* The safe course, if you can drive a manual at all, is simply to take the exam on a manual.

## 11. Fees

### Official examination fee

| Item | Net | Gross (19 % VAT) | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Practical exam, classes B / BE | €109.10 | **€129.83** | *GebOSt* Anlage, 3. Abschnitt, Nr. 402.3 |
| Theory exam, all classes (group exam, German or foreign language) | €21.00 (= €11.10 + €9.90 PC surcharge) | **€24.99** | *GebOSt* Anlage Nr. 401.1 + 401.3 |

VAT is added under *GebOSt* § 1 Abs. 3 and § 2 Abs. 1 Nr. 9. The amounts are **identical nationwide** and identical at DEKRA and TÜV — *GebOSt* is federal fee law, not a provider price list. There is no separate conversion fee number: an *Umschreibung* candidate pays exactly Nr. 402.3.

The €129.83 figure is cross-checked four ways: the *GebOSt* annex itself, DEKRA e.V. Dresden's official fee list (valid from 5 February 2024, covering Berlin), AS Fahrschule Berlin's price list (valid from 12 June 2026) and Turbo-Fahrschule Berlin. Effective since **31 January 2024** (a *GebOSt* amendment of roughly +11 %); `practical-exam.json` found **no 2025 or 2026 increase**. *GebOSt* was last amended by Art. 3 V v. 19.12.2025 (BGBl. 2025 I Nr. 382), but the research does not indicate that this touched Nr. 401 or 402.

**Outdated figures still circulating:** €116.93 practical / €22.49 or €91.75 — all pre-2024 and wrong. Expat sites in particular still quote them. allaboutberlin.com (updated 22 June 2026) already uses the correct rounded €130 / €25.

### Three *GebOSt* rules that can cost you money

From the *Vorbemerkung* / *Anmerkung* to Nr. 402:

1. **Do not book theory and practical on the same day.** If both parts are scheduled for one day at your request and you fail the theory part, *"wird für beide Prüfungsteile die volle Gebühr erhoben"* — you pay the full fee for both.
2. **A missed or aborted appointment without adequate excuse still costs the full fee** for the missed part.
3. If the practical exam is shortened under *Anlage 7* Nr. 2.3 or 2.5.1, the fee reduces proportionally. (Not applicable to you — see §2.)

### The driving school's *Vorstellungsentgelt*

This is the school's own charge for presenting you to the exam, and it is **not regulated**.

| Berlin school | Practical *Vorstellungsentgelt* |
|---|---|
| AS Fahrschule (conversion price page) | €220 |
| AS Fahrschule (general price list, from 12 June 2026) | €200 |
| Fahrschule CEM | €230 |
| Fahrschule PRIMA | €180 |
| National benchmark range | €150–300 |

**It is charged again on every retake.** Some schools charge a reduced €80–150 for a retake, others the full amount — ⚠️ unverified which applies at any given Berlin school; ask before you sign.

### Indicative total for the practical stage

| Component | Amount |
|---|---|
| Official practical exam fee | €129.83 |

## 12. Language: German only, and no interpreter

This is the hardest constraint on this route, and it is worth understanding *why* rather than just accepting it — because the reasoning tells you what you can and cannot ask for.

The foreign-language entitlement exists, but only for the theory exam:

> Die theoretische Prüfung ist in deutscher Sprache abzulegen und erfolgt anhand von Fragen. […] Abweichend von Satz 1 kann die Prüfung auch in folgenden Fremdsprachen abgelegt werden: a) Englisch, b) Französisch, c) Griechisch, d) Italienisch, e) Polnisch, f) Portugiesisch, g) Rumänisch, h) Russisch, i) Kroatisch, j) Spanisch, k) Türkisch, l) Hocharabisch.
> — *Anlage 7 Nr. 1.3 FeV*

Note the drafting: *"Die theoretische Prüfung"*. **The provision is about the theory exam and nothing else.** *Anlage 7 Nr. 2* (practical exam) and FeV § 17 contain no equivalent language provision and no interpreter provision at all, and VwVfG § 23 Abs. 1 makes German the official administrative language by default. So the practical exam is in German not because a rule forbids other languages, but because the exception that would permit them does not extend to it.

Two independent official confirmations: TÜV NORD states flatly that the practical test can be taken only in German and without an interpreter, giving an anti-fraud rationale. TÜV | DEKRA arge tp 21, the joint body that develops and runs the exams, confirms the 12-language list for the theory exam only.

**A widely-read source gets this wrong in the other direction.** settle-in-berlin.com claims that *both* exams must be in German. That is wrong for the theory exam, and contradicted by primary law, by TÜV | DEKRA arge tp 21 and by ADAC. For you this is good news: **your theory exam may be taken in Spanish, Italian or English, at the same fee** (DEKRA's own fee list prices *"Theoriepruefung Gruppenpruefung in Deutsch oder Fremdsprachen"* identically). Choose the language when the appointment is set up. Be warned that allaboutberlin.com — while correct on the 13 options — reports the translations are poor, a widely repeated criticism of the English and Spanish versions.

### What your instructor may and may not do

Your *Fahrlehrer* sits in the front passenger seat; the examiner sits behind you, specifically so that head movement is visible. Many Berlin schools advertise English-speaking instructors, and drilling the German command vocabulary during lessons is exactly what they are for. But during the exam the instructor **may not translate the examiner's instructions**. *Anlage 7 Nr. 2.5.3 FeV* provides that conduct making assessment of the candidate impossible, or attempting to deceive the examiner, causes the exam to be failed. Relaying instructions falls uncomfortably close to that line.

### What you *can* do — and should

How instructions are given is regulated, even though their wording is not:

> Vor Beginn der Prüfungsfahrt ist dem Bewerber zu erläutern, wie Anweisungen gegeben werden. Der aaSoP gibt die Fahrtstrecke an; erklärt sich der Bewerber als ortskundig, so können ihm mit seinem Einverständnis auch Fahrtziele vorgegeben werden. Im Übrigen kann der aaSoP Hinweise zum erwarteten Fahrverhalten geben, z. B. hinsichtlich der Geschwindigkeit.
> — *Prüfungsrichtlinie praktische Prüfung* Teil A Nr. 1.4.2

Three practical consequences:

1. **You get a briefing on how instructions will be given, before the drive starts.** Use it. Ask the examiner to speak slowly and to repeat when needed.
2. **Do not declare yourself *ortskundig* (familiar with the area).** Destination-based instructions ("drive to X") require your consent. Withhold it, and you get turn-by-turn directions instead — far easier to follow with limited German. Outside built-up areas you may still be told to navigate by direction signs (Nr. 1.4.3).
3. **Asking the examiner to repeat is never an error.** Learn one sentence cold: *"Könnten Sie das bitte wiederholen?"* German commentary stresses that instructions must be unambiguous, so that no misunderstanding about which junction is meant can arise — asking for clarification serves that requirement rather than undermining it.

One reassurance: the examiner is not permitted to quiz you on the meaning of traffic signs during the drive, nor to point out mistakes as you go (*Prüfungsrichtlinie* Nr. 1.4.4). The German you need is navigational and mechanical, not conversational.

`practical-exam.json` notes that many Berlin examiners will informally use simple English. **There is no entitlement to it.** Prepare as though there will be none.

## 13. Examiner command phrases

## 14. Berlin: exam centres, booking and waiting times

### Choosing your *Prüfstelle* — a decision you cannot undo

Berlin's application procedure is unusual and it interacts with this document directly. The application is filed at a *Bürgeramt* (citizens' office) or the LABO, **never at a driving school**, and it must already name both your driving school and your test centre:

> Name des Inhabers und Anschrift der Fahrschule sowie Name und Anschrift der Prüfstelle; **Nach Erhalt der Prüfungszulassung ist ein Wechsel der Prüfstelle nicht mehr möglich.**
> — service.berlin.de, Dienstleistung 327537

**Choose your *Prüfstelle* before you apply.** Once the *Prüfungszulassung* has issued you cannot change it. Not every Berlin driving school works with every *Prüfstelle*, so in practice you pick the school first and let it tell you which centre it uses. (If a caseworker enters the wrong *Fahrschule*, the LABO FAQ confirms that can be corrected.)

Booking then runs through the school. DEKRA states that *"Die Terminvereinbarung mit DEKRA erfolgt in der Regel über die Fahrschule."* Documents to bring to the exam: *Personalausweis* or *Reisepass* (ID card or passport) plus the school's *Ausbildungsbestätigung*.

⚠️ **Open question that specifically affects you:** what confirmation the *Prüfstelle* wants from a § 31 Abs. 2 candidate **in place of** an *Ausbildungsbescheinigung*, given that no training exists to certify. FeV § 16 Abs. 3 and § 17 Abs. 5 assume a training confirmation. Ask both the LABO and your *Prüfstelle* in writing: *"Welchen Nachweis muss ein Bewerber nach § 31 Abs. 2 FeV zur theoretischen und praktischen Prüfung mitbringen?"*

### Waiting times — the real bottleneck

Berlin has a structural backlog dating from COVID. Tagesspiegel reported roughly **20,000 accumulated practical exams**; waiting times for a practical slot have run to several months, with up to about six months reported in 2024/2025. berlin-live.de, updated 19 August 2025, still described waits of "months" and a persisting examiner shortage.

Counter-measures since 2024: guest examiners drawn from other *Bundesländer*, the *Bundespolizei* and *Bundeswehr*, reactivated instructors, Saturday exams, and roughly 4,700 additional exams per year. The Senate's target was to clear the backlog by the end of 2025, but shortage reports continued into 2025 and 2026.

Slots are released to driving schools online — reported as Thursdays at 11:00 — and are reported to be gone within seconds. **This makes your choice of driving school the single highest-leverage decision in the whole process**: a well-connected school with good slot access matters more than its hourly rate.

`practical-exam.json`'s planning advice: expect roughly **2–4+ months from being exam-ready to a practical slot** in Berlin, with retake slots typically 3–6 weeks.

⚠️ **Unverified:** the actual week-level waiting time as of mid-2026, after the backlog-clearing measures. Both files flag this as an open question. **Ask two or three Berlin schools directly what their current wait is — and ask before you commit to one.** This number should drive your timeline, and it interacts dangerously with the deadlines in §15 and with the six-month driving entitlement in §15.4.

## 15. Retakes and the deadlines that can wipe out your exam

### 15.1 The waiting period between attempts

> Bei Täuschungshandlungen gilt die Prüfung als nicht bestanden. Eine nicht bestandene Prüfung darf nicht vor Ablauf eines angemessenen Zeitraums, in der Regel mindestens zwei Wochen, wiederholt werden. In den Fällen des Satzes 1 kann die Frist für die Wiederholung der Prüfung auf bis zu neun Monate festgelegt werden.
> — FeV § 18 Abs. 1

So: **an adequate period, as a rule at least two weeks.** In Berlin the binding constraint is slot availability rather than the legal minimum — 3–6+ weeks is the reported reality.

On cheating: the statute provides for a repeat period of **up to nine months**. `practical-exam.json` describes the practice as "typically 6 weeks, up to 9 months for organized/repeated cheating" — ⚠️ **the six-week tier does not appear in the statutory text quoted above**, and `practical-exam.json` itself lists the precise wording of the § 18 blocking periods as an open question. Treat "up to nine months" as the reliable statement. For context, exam fraud rose 12 % to 4,198 cases in 2024 and the blocking periods are actively applied.

### 15.2 Number of attempts

**Unlimited.** FeV contains no cap for either exam; § 18 regulates only the waiting period. The old "three strikes then a three-month ban" rule no longer exists. Each attempt costs the €129.83 fee plus the *Vorstellungsentgelt* again (§11).

*Anlage 7 Nr. 1.2.3* requires a failed theory exam to be repeated in full. *Anlage 7 Nr. 2.5.1* provides that already-passed parts of a practical exam need not be repeated — relevant only to classes with separable parts, not to plain class B.

### 15.3 The two hard clocks

> Die praktische Prüfung muss innerhalb von zwölf Monaten nach Bestehen der theoretischen Prüfung abgelegt werden. Andernfalls verliert die theoretische Prüfung ihre Gültigkeit. Der Zeitraum zwischen Abschluss der praktischen Prüfung […] und der Aushändigung des Führerscheins darf zwei Jahre nicht überschreiten. Andernfalls verliert die gesamte Prüfung ihre Gültigkeit.
> — FeV § 18 Abs. 2

| Clock | Limit | Consequence of missing it |
|---|---|---|
| Theory pass → practical exam | **12 months** | Theory pass expires; retake it in full |
| Practical pass → licence handover | **2 years** | The entire examination loses validity |

Also FeV § 17 Abs. 1 Satz 5: the practical exam may not be taken before the theory exam is passed.

Combine the 12-month clock with Berlin's multi-month waiting times and the picture is uncomfortable: **do not pass your theory exam and then relax.** Get into the practical queue immediately.

### 15.4 Berlin grants no extension — and the gap is a criminal-law problem

The LABO FAQ is blunt on all three variants of the question:

- *"Meine Prüfzulassung ist abgelaufen, was muss ich tun? — Bitte stellen Sie einen neuen Antrag im Bürgeramt."*
- *"Meine Prüfzulassung für die theoretische Prüfung wird demnächst ablaufen, kann ich die Frist verlängern? — Nein."*
- *"Meine Prüfzulassung für die praktische Prüfung wird demnächst ablaufen, kann mir die Frist verlängert werden? — Fristverlängerungen werden nicht gewährt."*

If it lapses, you re-apply at the *Bürgeramt* and pay the authority fee again. (This is a *Land*-level practice, not federal: Hamburg operates a formal extension procedure. In Berlin, plan on none.)

⚠️ **Unverified and important:** how long the *Prüfungszulassung* / *Prüfauftrag* is itself valid in Berlin. **No official Berlin source states a number.** Secondary German sources conflict — one claims up to two years for the theory exam, another one year from the date of the letter. A genuine two-year rule that is often confused with it is the validity of the school's *Ausbildungsbescheinigung* (FeV § 16 Abs. 3 Satz 7: *"Der Abschluss der Ausbildung darf nicht länger als zwei Jahre zurückliegen"*) — which on your route may not even exist. **Ask the LABO in writing:** *"Wie lange ist die Prüfungszulassung für die theoretische und für die praktische Prüfung gültig, und ab welchem Datum läuft die Frist?"* Do not rely on any published number.

Why the deadlines are more than an administrative nuisance on your route: your Argentine licence entitles you to drive in Germany only for **six months** after establishing an *ordentlicher Wohnsitz* (FeV § 29 Abs. 1 Satz 4). Once that window closes and before your German *Fahrerlaubnis* issues, driving alone is *Fahren ohne Fahrerlaubnis* under StVG § 21 Abs. 1 Nr. 1 — punishable by up to one year's imprisonment or a fine. **An expired theory pass or *Prüfzulassung* therefore sits between you and legal driving, not merely between you and a licence card.** Lesson and exam drives with a *Fahrlehrer* remain covered (StVG § 2 Abs. 15). The § 29 Abs. 1 Satz 5 one-off extension requires you to show credibly that your German residence will last under twelve months — not available to a permanent Berlin resident. Verify the six-month baseline against the legal-core research; it is a cross-domain point.

## 18. Habits experienced non-EU drivers get wrong in Germany

This section is the reason an experienced Argentine driver can fail a German exam. Nothing here is about car control. Each item is anchored in the StVO, and each is on the guaranteed *Prüfort* list from §5.

### 18.1 *Rechts vor links* — and showing that you are yielding

> An Kreuzungen und Einmündungen hat die Vorfahrt, wer von rechts kommt.
> — StVO § 8 Abs. 1

Unless priority is regulated by signs (205, 206, 301, 306) or the other vehicle emerges from a field or forest track, **the vehicle from the right has priority** at unmarked junctions. Berlin side streets — very much including Friedrichshain — are full of these. Argentine practice, where priority normally follows signage or avenue hierarchy, is the classic trap.

There is a second layer, and it is where candidates actually lose points. StVO § 8 Abs. 2 requires you to *demonstrate* that you are yielding: you *"muss rechtzeitig durch sein Fahrverhalten, insbesondere durch mäßige Geschwindigkeit, erkennen lassen, dass gewartet wird"*. **Rolling up to an unmarked junction at unchanged speed is an error even if nothing is coming.** Visibly slow down, every time.

*Prüfort* requirement 8 is *"Befahren von Kreuzungen mit der Regelung 'rechts vor links'"* — it will occur on your route.

### 18.2 Roundabouts: do not indicate on entry

The counter-intuitive one. Where sign 215 (*Kreisverkehr*) appears under sign 205 (*Vorfahrt gewähren*), traffic already on the circle has priority, and StVO § 8 Abs. 1a provides:

> Bei der Einfahrt in einen solchen Kreisverkehr ist die Benutzung des Fahrtrichtungsanzeigers unzulässig.

**Indicating on entry is prohibited.** Indicating on **exit** is required by StVO § 9 Abs. 1. Many foreign drivers do precisely the opposite of both.

### 18.3 The double *Schulterblick*

This is a legal duty, not a driving-school ritual:

> Vor dem Einordnen und nochmals vor dem Abbiegen ist auf den nachfolgenden Verkehr zu achten; vor dem Abbiegen ist es dann nicht nötig, wenn eine Gefährdung nachfolgenden Verkehrs ausgeschlossen ist.
> — StVO § 9 Abs. 1 Satz 4

**Two checks: once before moving into position, once again before actually turning.** StVO § 5 Abs. 4 Satz 1 imposes the same duty before pulling out to overtake. Missing shoulder checks fall under the observation category *Verkehrsbeobachtung* and are described as **the single most-reported reason experienced drivers fail**.

### 18.4 Cyclists — three separate rules

**(a) Yield to straight-on cyclists when you turn.**

> Wer abbiegen will, muss entgegenkommende Fahrzeuge durchfahren lassen, Schienenfahrzeuge, Fahrräder mit Hilfsmotor, Fahrräder und Elektrokleinstfahrzeuge auch dann, wenn sie auf oder neben der Fahrbahn in der gleichen Richtung fahren.
> — StVO § 9 Abs. 3 Satz 1

Turning right across a cycle track means yielding to bikes going straight on — the opposite of many non-EU drivers' instinct. Satz 3 adds that particular consideration is owed to pedestrians, waiting if necessary. *Prüfort* requirement 13 is exactly this scenario, and Berlin's dense protected and painted cycle lanes mean it **will** be tested.

**(b) The 1.5-metre passing distance.**

> Beim Überholen mit Kraftfahrzeugen von zu Fuß Gehenden, Rad Fahrenden und Elektrokleinstfahrzeug Führenden beträgt der ausreichende Seitenabstand innerorts mindestens 1,5 m und außerorts mindestens 2 m.
> — StVO § 5 Abs. 4 Satz 3

**If the lane is too narrow to keep 1.5 m, you must stay behind the cyclist.** Squeezing past is a severe error. Note also § 5 Abs. 2 (overtake only at *wesentlich höherer Geschwindigkeit*) and § 5 Abs. 3 Nr. 1 (no overtaking *bei unklarer Verkehrslage*, in an unclear traffic situation).

**(c) Cyclists may legally overtake *you* on the right.**

> Ist ausreichender Raum vorhanden, dürfen Rad Fahrende und Mofa Fahrende die Fahrzeuge, die auf dem rechten Fahrstreifen warten, mit mäßiger Geschwindigkeit und besonderer Vorsicht rechts überholen.
> — StVO § 5 Abs. 8

So before moving off from a queue or turning right, check your **right-hand** blind spot and right mirror — not only the left.

### 18.5 Trams and bus stops — critical in eastern Berlin

Trams run through Friedrichshain, so this is not theoretical.

| Rule | Provision |
|---|---|
| Trams are overtaken **on the right** (on the left only if the rails lie too far right; on one-way carriageways also on the left) | StVO § 5 Abs. 7 Satz 2 |
| When turning left you may move onto longitudinal tram rails only if no tram is obstructed | StVO § 9 Abs. 1 Satz 3 |
| A tram or marked bus stopped at a *Haltestelle* (sign 224) may be passed, even in oncoming traffic, *"nur vorsichtig"* | StVO § 20 Abs. 1 |
| While passengers board or alight, you may pass on the right **only at walking pace** (*Schrittgeschwindigkeit*) and at a distance excluding any danger — *"Wenn nötig, muss, wer ein Fahrzeug führt, warten."* | StVO § 20 Abs. 2 |
| A bus or marked school bus approaching or standing at a stop with hazard lights on **may not be overtaken**; passing requires walking pace, including oncoming traffic on the same carriageway | StVO § 20 Abs. 3/4 |
| Buses must be let out of stops | StVO § 20 Abs. 5 |

*Prüfort* requirement 7 is *"Passieren von Haltestellen öffentlicher Verkehrsmittel"*.

### 18.6 Speed discipline cuts both ways

Everyone expects the speed limit. StVO § 3 Abs. 3 Nr. 1 sets 50 km/h *innerorts* for all motor vehicles even in the most favourable conditions, and § 3 Abs. 1 requires speed adapted to road, traffic, visibility and weather, allowing you to stop within the visible distance. § 3 Abs. 2a additionally demands reduced speed and readiness to brake around children, people needing assistance and older people.

What candidates do not expect is the other direction:

> Ohne triftigen Grund dürfen Kraftfahrzeuge nicht so langsam fahren, dass sie den Verkehrsfluss behindern.
> — StVO § 3 Abs. 2

**Driving 35 in a 50 zone out of caution is itself an error** under the observation category *Geschwindigkeitsanpassung*. Failing to *use* the available speed — in 30 zones, on the *Autobahn* — is described as a very common reason why experienced but cautious candidates fail. Drive at the limit where it is safe to do so.

### 18.7 Brake hard when told to

Restating §4 because it belongs in this list: *Abbremsen mit höchstmöglicher Verzögerung* is one of the three tasks from which two are drawn, and `practical-exam.json` reports it is almost always selected. **Experienced drivers habitually brake too gently and fail the task by not reaching maximum deceleration — by not triggering ABS.** Practise it until braking that hard on command feels normal.

### 18.8 Signal even when nobody is there

Restating from §6: *Kommunikation* is a scored category in its own right. Habitual non-signalling — lane changes, pulling out of a parking space, leaving a roundabout — or signalling too late is a scored competence gap, whether or not anyone was endangered (StVO § 9 Abs. 1 Satz 1, § 5 Abs. 4a).

## 19. Scheduled to change: the 2027 reform

**Status as of August 2026: draft, not law.** The federal cabinet adopted the *Fahrausbildung* reform package on **20 May 2026** under transport minister Patrick Schnieder. It has gone to the Bundestag and Bundesrat. The target is *"ein Inkrafttreten Anfang 2027"* — but the date is **not settled**: ADAC reports "early 2027", Deutsche Handwerks Zeitung (20 May 2026) gives 1 January 2027, and a third source states 1 July 2027 for the shorter practical exam. Contents can still change.

### What would affect you

| Change | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Practical exam shortened** | 55 → **40 minutes** total; pure *Fahrzeit* 30 → **25 minutes**. Parking manoeuvres reportedly not counted in the *Fahrzeit* |
| **Theory catalogue cut** | Reduced by about a third, to roughly **840 questions** |
| Scoring model | Reported switch to **one point per question** instead of the weighted 2–5 error points, with certain safety-critical questions that must not be answered wrongly — ⚠️ **reported by secondary press only, not yet reflected in *Anlage 7 FeV*** |
| Practical fee | *GebOSt* Nr. 402's rule that a shortened exam reduces the fee proportionally suggests the €129.83 would fall — ⚠️ unverified |
| B197 manual lessons | 10 → 7 (relevant only if B197 is available on your route — see §10) |

### What would not benefit you at all

Dropping the classroom attendance requirement for theory lessons, allowing app-based theory instruction, making the 12 *Sonderfahrten* flexible or cutting them to three (one each *Überland*, *Autobahn*, *Nacht*), permitting simulators, abolishing rules on classrooms and teaching materials, and *Fahrschule* price transparency via the Mobilithek. **§ 31 Abs. 2 FeV already exempts you from every training rule, so the headline savings of this reform are worth exactly zero on the conversion route.** These are savings for the first-time-licence persona.

There is also a five-year pilot (*Experimentierklausel*) for accompanied lay practice — reportedly 1,000 km with a family member after a minimum number of lessons or after passing the theory exam. ⚠️ Whether this would extend to § 31 Abs. 2 conversion candidates is unverified. If it did, it would be a cheap way to build German road experience, so it is worth watching.

### Should you wait? No.

Four reasons, and they compound:

1. **The required knowledge level will not fall.** It is fixed by EU Directive 2006/126/EG; ADAC states the required standard remains unchanged because it is EU-prescribed. A shorter exam is not an easier exam.
2. **It is not law yet, and the date is disputed** (early 2027 vs 1 January 2027 vs 1 July 2027).
3. **Your six-month driving entitlement runs out long before** (FeV § 29 Abs. 1 Satz 4). Waiting means either not driving or committing an offence under StVG § 21.
4. **A new question catalogue may obsolete what you have already learned**, so delay has its own cost.

The BMDV's own advice, reported by auto-motor-und-sport, is not to wait for the reform before starting.
